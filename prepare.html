Preparing for Your Visit

A few things to know before your appointment so we can make the most of your time.

What to Know Before Your Visit

Bringing the right records and wearing the right clothing changes how much can be accomplished at a first appointment.

If you have had imaging done anywhere else — bring both

Bring a CD or USB drive containing the actual images, and a printed or PDF copy of the imaging report. One without the other is frequently not enough. The report tells us what a radiologist concluded; the images let us look ourselves.

For prior EMGs or previous surgeries, bring the procedure note or operative report from that surgeon, along with any post-operative instructions and any other imaging you have.

Imaging on the day

Simpler imaging such as X-rays and ultrasound can often be done the day of your appointment. Advanced imaging such as MRI and CT is usually not available same-day and generally needs to be scheduled separately, sometimes pending insurance authorization.

What to wear

For upper extremity problems we need to examine the entire arm and shoulder, and occasionally the neck. Please wear clothing that comes off easily — a loose button-down shirt is ideal. Tight sleeves make a proper exam difficult.

Costs and coverage

You may have a co-pay, and what you owe can vary depending on which services are provided at the visit. Contact your insurance provider ahead of time to understand your coverage.

If you are hoping for surgery or an injection

There may be additional treatment steps required before a procedure can be scheduled — imaging, a course of therapy, or documentation your insurer requires. The first visit is often about establishing that pathway rather than performing the procedure itself.

Disability, FMLA, and insurance paperwork

Paperwork generally cannot be completed during your appointment. Send it ahead of time or leave it with us afterwards, and allow a two-week window for it to be completed and returned.

Fax it to the office and call to let us know it is coming, so it does not sit unnoticed.

Language support

Care is provided in English and Spanish. A translator can be arranged for other languages — please request one when scheduling so it is in place before you arrive.
